00 · Abstract

The IP address says where a packet is going. The MAC address says where it goes next.

The coursework looks at one network from two sides. First by watching it: a Wireshark capture on macOS, read through six display filters. A single ping produced ten ICMP packets, and the first echo request was framed for the router's MAC, not Google's.

Then by building one. In Packet Tracer, 199.19.30.0/24 was split into four /26 subnets, and a Cisco 2901 router joined two of them. The first ping failed with 100% loss because the two switch uplinks were on swapped router ports. After re-cabling, replies came back with TTL=127: one hop.

04 · Two clocks

        
The wire took 78.52 ms. The app counted 121.913.

        

          Wireshark stamps a packet when it passes the network interface.
          ping reads the clock inside the program, so it also counts
          the kernel, socket buffers and the time it takes to be scheduled. That
          overhead was about 43 ms on the first echo and only
          1 to 2 ms on the other three.
